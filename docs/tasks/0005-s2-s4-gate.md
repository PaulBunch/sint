## Decision

| Role | Scheme |
|------|--------|
| **MVP primary** | **S4** — LIMS-inspired serial 6-DoF (`docs/tasks/0005-candidate-scheme-s4.md`) |
| **Fallback** | **S2** — cascaded serial 6-DoF (`docs/tasks/0004-candidate-schemes.md` §2) |
| **Last-resort distal** | **S1** in-joint distal only if cascade / through-elbow paths both fail DFAA or N6 (unchanged spirit of ADR-0005) |

**ADR-0005** is **amended** (not silently replaced): S4 becomes the MVP implementation primary; S2 remains the documented cascade baseline and explicit fallback. Full dual-ended **S3** stays deferred.

This gate does **not** freeze motor SKUs, pretension hardware, or full-arm COTS (task 0005 C2–C6 continue under S4-primary mapping, with S2 delta noted).

---

## Rationale (short)

1. **Project goal** is not maximum short-term simplicity. Identical in-joint modules (GLUON-class) optimize repeatability, not the structural limits of classic positioner arms.  
2. **Scorecard (C1b.5):** S4 leads on **distal inertia** and **dock-neighbourhood / fold** workspace; S2 leads on integration speed and specification maturity of the cascade story. Maturity asymmetry is acknowledged (S4 wrist/elbow more detailed than S2 today).  
3. **ADR-0006:** LIMS-class dynamics are the performance *direction* under DFAA and ADR-0003 BOM/torque bands — not a claim of parity with any commercial product.  
4. **Relocatable (“walking”) function** remains first-class: planar ≥2 docks, Scenario A, base aux-latch walk; S4 must improve approach/fold around docks without requiring S3 dual-ended ends in Phase 1.  
5. **Risk control:** S4 MVP is **narrow** (below); **fallback triggers** to S2 are pre-declared; critical mechanisms validated **inside MVP**, not a separate pre-gate CAD contest. Printed gears/rollers allowed for mechanism bring-up.

---

## Narrow MVP S4 scope

**In scope for first integrated arm article:**

- Structure: **base** (dock interface only) → **J1** → **shoulder yoke** (J2 + J3 motors) → **L2 upper arm** (compute, optional transit battery, wrist pack) → **dual-hinge J3** via **rigid link** → **light L3** → **bevel 3-DoF wrist**  
- Through-elbow **open** cable or belt media; agent-reachable covers  
- Pretension: **simplest serviceable** option chosen in CAD (manual turnbuckle acceptable); exotic single-pretension EX clone not required  
- Power Scenario A; asymmetric ends; planar dual-dock walk + base aux-latch  
- ADR-0003 numeric bands (0.5 kg class payload, torque/acoustic/BOM intent)

**Explicitly out of narrow MVP:**

- Full LIMS-EX sealed routing / undocumented N+1-to-2N copy  
- Non-coplanar climb / wrist-as-hoist walk  
- S3 dual-ended symmetry  
- Custom frameless motors as a requirement  
- ALLEX or any third-party product parity claims  

---

## Fallback triggers → S2 (or simplify in place)

| Trigger (observed on MVP path) | Response |
|--------------------------------|----------|
| Through-elbow binding or pretension not tunable after agreed iteration budget | Wrist pack / media → **S2-style** short cascade on forearm **or** simplify media |
| Dual-hinge not assemblable / calibratable within DFAA-reasonable service time | **Single-hinge** elbow; keep proximal packs if possible |
| N6 or wear of printed mesh unacceptable and no COTS substitute in budget | Simplify wrist mesh or fall back toward **S2** belt narrative |
| BOM / calendar blow-up without measurable fold or distal-inertia benefit | Declare **S2 primary** via short ADR note |
| Cascade path itself fails DFAA/N6 | Consider **S1** distal only (last resort) |

Fallback does not require shame; it is part of the plan. Record the trigger in conversation + current-state when fired.

---

## Directions to preserve in language (not hype)

When updating ROADMAP / current-state / composition ADR, prefer:

1. **LIMS-class motion direction** — low distal inertia, smooth quiet motion, backdrivability *intent* — under **DFAA** and **ADR-0003 BOM/torque**.  
2. **Relocatable / walking direction** — planar multi-dock mobility as a core capability, improved by S4 fold where possible, without Phase 1 climb.  
3. Avoid product-ranking claims about external robots.
